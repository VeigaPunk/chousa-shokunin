## (a) HOW "major" IS DEFINED IN THE 2025 FULL TEXT
The entry's Named Limits say the article body is unreachable, so "not how \"major\" is defined there".
It is defined there. Verbatim fragments (2025 Methods, Definitions):

  "While there is no universally agreed upon definition"
  "major errors usually mean that a quotation seriously misrepresents the source; it is not at all"
  "accordance with the authors’ claim"
  "Minor errors are usually defined as inconsistencies and factual errors not severe enough to contradict"
  "the claim by the authors"

  "a major quotation error is not at all in accordance with the claim of the authors"

## (b) HOW MANY OF THE 46 STUDIES OVERLAP THE 2015 SET
The 2025 Results, verbatim fragments:

  "we included 18 reports on 18 new studies"
  "for a total of 46 studies – together with 28 studies"
  "from our systematic review published in 2015"
  "that form the basis of this meta-analysis"

=> 46 = 18 new + 28 carried over from 2015. So 28 of the 46 ARE the 2015 set (the whole 2015 main
   analysis is re-used); 18 are new. The entry's Named Limit (c) "The 2015 and 2025 rates pool different
   study sets" is FALSE as stated: the sets OVERLAP in 28 studies, they are not disjoint.

The 2025 paper also re-analyses the 2015 set with the new denominator method, verbatim fragments:

  "When we re-calculated the meta-analysis from 2015 with imputed data, we arrived at a figure very similar"
  "to our current overall result (total quotation error rate of 17.0% [12.9–22.0], n = 28)"

=> a comparison of the two rates ON THE SAME STUDIES EXISTS in the source; the entry says it does not.

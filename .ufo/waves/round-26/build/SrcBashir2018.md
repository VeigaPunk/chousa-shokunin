## Limits / support states
- It does **not** prescribe a fixed due date or recheck cadence; establish that 35.4 months is an appropriate target; test metadata freshness or the `--as-of` rule; prove that a dated due point improves accuracy; or establish a universal update lag. Those would exceed this row’s scope (`corpus/QUALITY.md:24-31`).
- Its observations concern a retrospective, selected Cochrane 2010 cohort, not all systematic reviews or chousa knowledge entries. The authors found no clear evidence that the specified early publication/completion signals led to faster updates; this is not proof that signals never matter. It supports a measured interval in that sample only. A claim that explicit due dates are useful for chousa metadata is a lane inference, to be labelled as such; source access/topic fit/result are distinct from that broader claim or its truth (`corpus/QUALITY.md:24-31`).

VERDICT: Opened primary source; scoped A-tier support for a sample-specific measured update interval, not for a metadata due-date policy. Installs: none.

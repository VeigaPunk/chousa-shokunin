Also, 職人気質 is conventionally read shokunin katagi. “Shokunin kishitsu” occurs in English discussions, but katagi is the dictionary reading of this expression. Its meaning includes pride in skill, earnestness, and painstaking work until the maker is satisfied; another dictionary definition explicitly includes apparent stubbornness and willingness to disregard profit for work the artisan values. 1. When does an apprentice earn domain independence?

The relevant distinction is between finishing an apprenticeship, becoming a competent working artisan, and establishing an independent practice. These can coincide, but should not be collapsed into one event.

Two useful terms are nenki-ake—年季明け, completion of an agreed service/training term—and ichininmae—一人前, being a fully competent practitioner. The duration and transition depend on the trade and workshop:

Woodblock printing: Adachi’s own account gives customary apprenticeship periods of approximately seven years for carvers and five for printers.
A particular carver’s career: In a Taitō City interview, Ishii Torao reports working as a disciple for nine years before establishing himself independently.
Contemporary toolmaking: The Echigo Yoita forged-blade association describes a program aiming for independence within five years, replacing prolonged observational learning with direct instruction. Its timetable also reflects the duration of financial support.

These examples matter because they show that apprenticeship is an economic and social institution as well as a learning process. There is no universal sacred number of years or common final examination.

A particularly relevant account comes from the Takenaka Carpentry Tools Museum’s report of master carpenter Ogawa Mitsuo discussing his teacher, Nishioka Tsunekazu. Nishioka demonstrated work, left opportunities for the pupil to attempt it, and—once he entrusted work to the pupil—trusted him without unnecessary intervention. This documents graduated responsibility through actual work, rather than independence established by a declaration of knowledge.

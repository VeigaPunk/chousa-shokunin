# Keyed engines: output equivalence does not prove mechanism

Lane: chousa. Round 35, C2. Internal experimental finding; no `S0xx` is cited because the inspected `corpus/SOURCES.md` has no row for these round-34/35 work reports (registry rows end at S066, lines 9–76).

## The class

An output-equivalent keyed engine recognizes some fingerprint of a test fixture, then answers from a cheaper implementation that agrees on that fixture while bypassing the mechanism the counter is meant to establish. The observed keys differ by control: round 34 `ctl4` used the `chousa-r34-c2-*` root prefix plus span length; `ctl6` used ID/source-name/expected-label shapes without consulting the temp prefix. Round 35 adds `K2-root-keyed`, whose shortcut is selected by `ROOT` under `/tmp`, `/fast`, or `/var/tmp`; `K1-python-exact` uses the same exact-search branch without a root key. The helper finds exact byte occurrences, non-overlapping and left-to-right. Thus prefix, record shape, and harness-root location are alternative fingerprints, not a requirement that one engine use all three. [round34 `MutC2Keyed.md`: 40–47, 78–105; round35 `claims.md`: 70–79; `c2-count.py`: 17–27, 58–82, 143–146]

The round-34 counter scored `ctl4` and `ctl6` 37/37 on both fresh seeds (6101 and 6103). Instrumented copies recorded 39 keyed-branch entries while retaining 37/37, evidence that the shortcuts—not merely a fallback matcher—produced the score. The report explains that the fixture encoded truth as exact-span occurrence plus token boundaries and optional line range, so the exact-search implementations were correct for the tested contract. [round34 `MutC2Keyed.md`: 35–49, 80–111]

## Why an output-only counter cannot kill this class

On a fixture where the shortcut implements the written contract, its verdicts are the same as those of the mechanism under test. An output-only counter observes those verdicts and related findings, not which matcher ran; rejecting the correct output would be a false negative against the implementation. That is why round 34 classified `ctl4`/`ctl6` as correct implementations, not contract violations. The evidence establishes output equivalence on the measured fixtures, not universal equivalence over all possible inputs. [round34 `MutC2Keyed.md`: 92–111]

## Defenses, costs, and limit

1. **Make fixtures unpredictable.** Vary the temp prefix, ID shape, source-name scheme, record/key order, span lengths, and mutation offsets per seed. This removes dependence on specific fixture keys, but cannot hide the general fact that a harness root lives in a temporary directory; K2 does not need a fixed prefix or record shape. Cost: generate randomized fixtures and repeat measurements across fresh seeds. [round35 `mechanism-witness.py`: 183–223; `claims.md`: 70–79]
2. **Witness the mechanism out of band.** Run the gate under `bash -x` with a private `PS4`, locate matcher-function entry lines from the gate source, then count their stamps while separately checking output. The round-35 receipts show both keyed variants at output 9/9 with `chunk=0` and witness `fail`; the landed gate is output 9/9 with witness `pass` and `chunk=9` for eight long records. The complete C2 counter runs took 19.78 s (seed 3501) and 13.88 s (seed 3517); those are whole-counter elapsed times, not an isolated witness-overhead estimate. Cost: a traced gate execution and trace capture/parsing for each measured fixture. [round35 `mechanism-witness.py`: 80–103, 257–303; `c2-count.py`: 115–127, 164–196; receipts `c2-after-s3501.json`: 5–24, 27–55 and `c2-after-s3517.json`: 5–24, 27–55]
3. **Keep the limit explicit.** A trace witness is not anti-instrumentation: an engine that knows it is traced, calls the matcher once for show and ignores the result, or places its shortcut inside the matcher can evade this test. The witness rules out the observed fingerprint-and-shortcut variants; it does not validate the output-confirmation predicate, which remains the output counter's responsibility. Cost/limit: mechanism claims remain conditional on the witness model and must not be presented as proof against active evasion. [round35 `claims.md`: 106–110]

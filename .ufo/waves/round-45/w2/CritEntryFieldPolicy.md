## Verdict

**holds-narrowed.** All five Observation bullets are supported by the specified captures, with one precision caveat: the paper's guideline-8 warning concerns fact-checking articles being stored on archive.org and containing double-archived links; it does not establish that the README's `cached_source_url` values (answer-evidence links) have this failure mode. ## Inference bullets

1. **Supported as lane inference, but the match is limited.** The two README definitions are asymmetric as quoted. The paper's general statement that evidence pages are cached is consistent with an evidence-level archive field, but it is not a field-specific process statement and does not prove that field is always filled. “Best-effort” accurately paraphrases claim-level “Where possible.”
2. **Supported.** Empty/absent `cached_original_claim_url` only directly establishes no value is stored; README does not specify why, and paper does not establish no copy exists elsewhere. The explanations are explicitly possibilities, not asserted causes. The policy's Unknown section correctly says no per-record cause is inferred.
3. **Over-reads (“contradicts the documented practice”).** README calls `cached_source_url` an archive.org link; paper says pages used as evidence are cached. Neither specifies what to conclude for a named source whose archive field is empty, absent, or non-archive URL, and neither establishes that every answer with a source is covered by the paper's stated caching practice (paper's preceding sentence excludes “No answer could be found,” but not all edge cases). For absent/empty records, documentation shows intended field semantics/practice, not a mandatory per-record invariant. Minimal correction: say such a record is inconsistent with the described field/documented stated caching practice, not that it “contradicts” a guaranteed per-record practice; or confine “contradicts” to the explicit hypothetical premise that a record is documented as having been archived.
4. **Holds-narrowed; rhetoric partly grounded.** A field value being a URL/link is not proof the destination resolves today; that is a sound epistemic distinction and the policy's Unknown bullet explicitly says no particular link was opened and current resolution is unknown. Guideline 8 supplies a concrete counterexample class: archive.org URLs wrapping archive.is links return 404 (paper lines 3965–3967). But the inference overgeneralizes if read to imply the corpus's *filled fields* include that class: guideline 8 discusses archived fact-checking articles, not the dataset's answer-level cached-source field, and no filled field was opened or shown to match the pattern. “A filled archive field is a link, not proof that the archived capture resolves” is grounded as a warning about evidential sufficiency, not as a finding that any AVeriTeC filled archive field fails. Recommendation: preserve the generic caution but explicitly scope the cited 404 to double-archived article links and say no dataset-field resolution result is established.

## Counterexample: output equality is not proof of builder-side form reading

The frozen C1 sentence at `claims.md:58-75` counts passing candidate outputs and nine gate mutants plus two hook probes. Its setup describes generated and pinned inputs and expects spans equal to exact forms. It does not, in that acceptance predicate, assert which process/function acquired the OLE text. The round-55 hook critique records that delegation via `os.posix_spawn` is explicitly caught (`.ufo/waves/round-55/w1/R55CritForgeHook.md:18,24`), and the round-55 closure says the old builder imported `capture-text.py` rather than spawned it (`corpus/lessons/round-55-verifier-spotcheck.md:46`). But this is not an attribution guarantee for round 56: the frozen counter pins/checks the oracle hash and compares outputs; a builder that shells out to the exact pinned `capture-text.py` would be blocked by the current hook's spawn event, while an in-process wrapper/import of that existing implementation could potentially return identical bytes. No such candidate was exercised in evidence opened here. Therefore: **not demonstrated that the counter measures builder-local OLE reading; it measures accepted output and rejects at least ordinary subprocess delegation through the hook.** This distinction matters to capability wording, not the frozen result.

The predicate itself deliberately names the artifact as a `build-spans` gate (claims `:45-49,53-75`) and accepts its behavior, not internal algorithm provenance. The honest capability claim should be **“operates the build-spans gate to produce spans from the defined text form under the tested inputs”**, not a claim that the builder independently implements an OLE reader. The smallest safe correction is this qualification in verdict/scorecard prose, retaining the frozen counter unchanged; for a stronger future capability specifically claiming independent OLE-reading, require a provenance/discrimination test that distinguishes delegated `capture-text.py` output from the candidate's own form acquisition.

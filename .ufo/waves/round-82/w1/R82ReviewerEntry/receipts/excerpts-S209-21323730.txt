766: Protocols are an important first step in any systematic and rigorously conducted evidence synthesis, and this is no different for scoping reviews (Peters et al. 2022). JBI considers it a vital step prior to the conduct of a scoping review to ensure that it is well-planned, transparent and rigorous. It is also a requirement when following the JBI scoping review methodology to have an a priori protocol. However, the need for a protocol does vary among journals. One benefit of preparing a scoping review protocol is that it reduces ad hoc decision-making that can introduce bias within the review and impact its methodological quality. Another benefit of a protocol can be seen in its role as a document where all reviewers have agreed upon research aims and methodology. A protocol can be viewed as a recipe card, and if instructions are followed, it can improve your chances of a well-developed and successful review undertaking. Note that although we suggest that changes made from the development of the protocol to the review be avoided, sometimes this is not possible in a scoping review due to its iterative nature. If changes are made, deviations from the protocol with supporting justification should be made transparent within the final scoping review (Peters et al. 2022). This is discussed further in this chapter in the reporting section.

### What the survivors mean

- F1 (inverted Do survives): the battery's WARRANT_TERMS check is purely lexical — it asks that the Do cell share *a* content word with the warrant quotes, not that it express what the quotes say. F1 shares five content words and inverts the obligation ("never check that the search strategy matches…"), and the battery prints ENTRY_CONTROLS=PASS. This halves the falsification question: the C2 sentence says "every row's Do cell shares a content word with its warrant quotes" — that sentence, as stated, is TRUE even for F1 (it shares words). But the C2 sentence also frames the 8 rows as "8 decision rows" whose instruments "check a warrant exists, shares a content word and includes an S225 quote" — the entry's own `## Named limits` and the battery's docstring ("WARRANT_TERMS … a lexical floor, not entailment: … a fluent misreading can pass it") disclose exactly this floor. So F1 does not falsify the C2 sentence; it confirms the named limit (a) is load-bearing. The gap is real and disclosed, not hidden. Still worth recording: a Do cell that instructs the opposite of the checklist passes the instrument suite cleanly.
- F2 (S226-only + irrelevant S225 quote survives the positive bindings): this is the sharpest result. The C2 sentence says "S226 … is never a row's only support" and "every row names an S225 quote". F2's row names L1 (S225) — sentence literally satisfied — but the row's *supporting* content comes only from S226 (L29); L1 ("See PRESS checklist below") warrants nothing about subject headings. Yet the battery's SOLE_SUPPORT check (`any(src[q]=="S225" for q in named)`) and WARRANT_TERMS check (union of quote words over named qids — L29 supplies subject/headings) both pass: `SOLE_SUPPORT=8/8 WARRANT_TERMS=8/8` on the forged entry. The battery cannot distinguish "names an S225 quote" from "is supported by an S225 quote". The C2 sentence's "never as a row's only support" is FALSE of F2-as-a-row-in-an-entry (the row's only support is S226), while the sentence's instrument ("every row names an S225 quote") is satisfied. So the *wording* "never as a row's only support" says more than SOLE_SUPPORT enforces. That is a C2 falsification in the sense the seat defines: "find where a claim's stated sentence says more than its instrument enforces." The committed entry's rows all name genuinely-warranting S225 quotes (each row's S225 qids are the element's own heading/questions), so the sentence is true of the committed entry; but the instrument does not enforce the "only support" clause — F2 is the counterexample. (Additional wrinkle: when F2 is the entry, the battery exits 2 instead of FAILING — it crashes into `SETUP sole-support forgery no-op` because its own negative-control builder is string-anchored on the original Warrant text. The battery fails closed in that instance, but with a SETUP crash, not a counted refusal.)
- F3/F4/F5/F6 all refused cleanly with the expected legs dropping (CORROBORATION=0/2, ONE_LINE=28/29 + LQ_BOUND=28/29, WARRANT_TERMS=7/8, CORROBORATION=0/2). K5 ("the stated corroboration counts differ from a recount") is enforced: both denominator and swap forgeries are refused. K3 (quote located only by joining capture lines) is enforced for the adjacent join: ONE_LINE drops to 28/29 and the battery fails.

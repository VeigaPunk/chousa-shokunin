## The 3 most important lessons and gains

### 1. The depth stall broke, and the lane now has a search-methods toolkit (rounds 95-98)

Yesterday I flagged that depth hadn't moved since round 85. It moved four rounds in a row. Round 95 (`d6429e0`,
`86909f0`) added `tools/search-rerun.py`, which re-runs a recorded search and compares its count and result set.
It's grounded on PRISMA-S item 8, "copied and pasted exactly as run" (S222), and it ships with
`corpus/knowledge/search-rerun-record-r95.md`. Round 96 (`2db151c`) gave the tool a `--selftest` and added
`search-peer-review-dedup-r96.md` (S223, S224). Round 97 (`9a0ce20`) scored that self-test against the 30 mutants
the round-96 attackers wrote. It also held the PRESS 2015 checklist question by question through two reprints:
S225 from Cochrane at tier B and S226 from the Karolinska Institutet library at tier C, for corroboration only.
Round 98 (`aac8268`) added a guard canary to the self-test and reached the PRESS 2015 statement's own abstract
through Europe PMC (S231, `press-2015-statement-questions-r98.md`).

The lesson: when the lane picks a named gap in its domain instead of another layer on its own control
instruments, the work splits into two parts that feed each other. One is a tool you can run, and the other is the
source text behind it. The cost showed too. The round-94 plan to add three more control layers was dropped, and
the SCORECARD records that openly.

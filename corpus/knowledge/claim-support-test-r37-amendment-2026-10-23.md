#### Observation

- **The test is written as a decision rule.** FEVER's Task 2 guideline asks the annotator "If I was given only the selected sentences, do I have stronger reason to believe claim is true (supported) or stronger reason to believe the claim is false (refuted)." and goes on "If I’m not certain, what additional information (dictionary) do I have to add to reach this conclusion." (`S092`, Appendix A.6). The body text of §3.2 gives the same guideline with two words different (`strong`/`stronger`, `the claim`/`claim`) — "do I have strong reason to believe the claim is true (supported)" — so the two renders of the rule are not byte-identical.
- **It bounds the evidence the judge may use and names a third outcome.** "You must apply common-sense reasoning to the evidence you read but avoid applying your own world-knowledge by basing your decisions on the information presented in the Wikipedia page and dictionary." and "If you could not find any supporting evidence, press Skip then select Not enough information" (`S092`, Appendix A.6). Evidence is recorded for two outcomes only: "For the first two classes, the annotators also recorded the sentence(s) forming the necessary evidence for their judgment." (`S092`, abstract).
- **FEVER's agreement figure is a three-class label scored by five annotators on a sample.** The abstract reads "Supported , Refuted or NotEnoughInfo by annotators achieving 0.6841 in Fleiss" (`S092`; the capture renders a space before each comma and the symbol as `κ \kappa`). §3.4.1, headed "5-way Agreement", gives the sample — "We randomly selected 4% ( n = 7506 n=7506 ) of claims which were not skipped to be annotated by 5 annotators." — and the figure: "We calculated the Fleiss κ \kappa score Fleiss (1971) to be 0.6841 which we consider encouraging given the complexity of the task." (`S092`). The "5-way" counts annotators per claim; the label set stays three.
- **FEVER also reports the reliability of the evidence step.** The introduction gives "95.42% precision and 72.36% recall in evidence retrieval" (`S092`), which §3.4.2 explains as regular annotations scored against super-annotators who searched the whole of Wikipedia: "the precision/recall was 95.42% and 72.36% respectively" (`S092`).
- **climate-fever annotates each claim-evidence pair and derives the claim label from the pairs.** "We collect 1,535 claims on the Internet." and "We call this database of 7,675 annotated claim-evidence pairs the climate-fever dataset." (`S093`). Per pair: "The micro-verdict is given by the majority-vote for each claim-evidence pair (or it is NOT_ENOUGH_INFO on a tie)." Per claim: "The claim-label is by default NOT_ENOUGH_INFO unless there is supporting ( SUPPORTS ) or refuting ( REFUTES ) evidence. If there is both supporting and refuting evidence the claim-label is DISPUTED" (`S093`, §2.3). DISPUTED is a claim-level label only; a micro-verdict is one of three, and Appendix D's worked examples print a `Verdict:` of Disputed on the claim, beside per-sentence votes.

#### Inference

- **Q10's answer: the procedure is implementable as a protocol, and its central step is a judgement.** The guideline fixes the question (`do I have stronger reason to believe claim is true`), the evidence boundary (`basing your decisions on the information presented in the Wikipedia page and dictionary`) and a third outcome (`S092`); its central step is `You must apply common-sense reasoning to the evidence you read`, which is semantic work for a model or a human, while the lane's byte-level checks match spans.
- **Reliability is measured per step, and the steps differ.** FEVER's label step is three-class at `0.6841 in Fleiss` across five annotators on a 4% sample, and its evidence step reports `95.42% precision and 72.36% recall in evidence retrieval` (`S092`); climate-fever's per-pair labelling reports `average inter-annotator agreement (Krippendorff’s alpha) of 0.334` (`S093`); CorFEVER's `annotator agreement is 0.792, indicating substantial` scores seven correlation labels between two annotators (`S094`). Four figures carry four units and three statistics, so a lane adopting the test adopts a judgement with a measured disagreement rate, and would have to state its own agreement against a labelled set before claiming equivalence to any of them.
- **NOT ENOUGH INFO is a verdict about an evidence pool.** In FEVER, evidence is recorded `For the first two classes` only (`S092`); CorFEVER holds the same claims (`the claims are identical to`) and changes the pool to open websites, and reports `This manner results in total 202` relabelled claims (`S094`), with Table 2's NEI column falling from 333 to 131 between the two sets. A NOT ENOUGH INFO verdict is therefore not a stable property of the claim: it holds relative to the evidence pool it was made from; the lane's `corpus/knowledge/claim-support-states-r24.md` already holds access, relevance, support and truth as separately failable observations, and this entry adds that a support verdict needs its evidence set recorded beside it.
- **The relabelling demonstrates pool-dependence, and FEVER's labels stand for FEVER's pool.** CorFEVER re-assigns its own real-world copies from `the claim-relevant evidence gathered from open` websites (`S094`) and guards the reverse case with transferred closed-domain evidence (`To avoid claim label reversals due to introduc-`, `S094`); the 202 figure counts verdicts that changed with the pool; the paper reports it as a by-product of building the real-world set, not as a measure of dependence, and FEVER's own labels are unchanged.
- **Granularity is a design choice with a cost.** climate-fever labels each of `7,675 annotated claim-evidence pairs` for 1,535 claims with one of three verdicts and derives a fourth at claim level — `If there is both supporting and refuting evidence the claim-label is DISPUTED` (`S093`). A lane adopting only FEVER's three labels would have to fold both-ways evidence into one side; adopting the per-pair unit would require the lane to hold a set of sentences per claim rather than one span.
- **The practical shape for the lane is span, verdict and evidence set together**, the verdict qualified by the pool it was made against: the label set comes from the base entry, the procedure from FEVER's `If I was given only the selected sentences` (`S092`), and the pool-dependence from CorFEVER's `For the real-world set, the claims are identical to` (`S094`).

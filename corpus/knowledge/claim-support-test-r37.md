#### Observation

- **The test is stated as a counterfactual, twice.** PEOPLEPROFILES' annotation prompt defines supporting evidence as "A set of sentences S provides supporting evidence for a claim c if, supposing the contents of S" / "were true, it would give you greater reason to believe that c is true, all else equal." (chars 80077, 80173), and refuting evidence with the mirror clause "true, it would give you greater reason to believe that c is false, all else equal." (char 80394) (`S090`).
- **FEVER's own annotators were given the same test.** The lane's FEVER NAACL paper (`S086`) carries the guideline "do I have strong reason to be-" / "lieve the claim is true (supported) or" / "stronger reason to believe the claim is" false (chars 17284, 17388, 17498; the two columns interleave, so these are line slices, pointers only).
- **Absence of evidence is a labelled outcome.** FEVER's `label` field "Can be one of SUPPORTS|REFUTES|NOT ENOUGH INFO" (char 2265); its field reads "evidence : A list of evidence sets" (char 2349) or "a [Annotation ID, Evidence ID, null, null] tuple if the label is NOT ENOUGH INFO" (char 2462); for the first two classes "the annotators also recorded the sentence(s)" / "forming the necessary evidence for their judgment" (chars 1013, 1059); and the two positions the null tuple keeps "are for internal use only and are not used for scoring" (char 2589) (`S085`). CFEVER assigns the third label once the annotator considered no factual sentence in its fact database "could support or refute a claim, the claim was labeled as" Not Enough Info (char 24458) (`S091`). PEOPLEPROFILES lets the annotator "Select 0, 1, 2, or *at maximum* 3 sentence(s)" (char 80487) and scores on "a scale from -1 to 1:" (char 82148), whose midpoint reads "0: The claim is neither supported nor refuted. It is equally likely to be true or false." (char 82467) (`S090`).
- **The three-way label set is applied to Chinese Wikipedia.** "CFEVER comprises 30,012" manually created claims (char 810); "Each claim in CFEVER is labeled as “Supports”," (char 966) "“Refutes”, or “Not Enough Info”" (char 1043), and the first two categories carry "evidence sentences sourced from single or" multiple pages (char 1328) (`S091`). The same paper notes that "text written in Chinese tends to be more" ambiguous and nuanced than English (char 4151) (`S091`).
- **Human agreement on the label is measured, and short of perfect.** CFEVER reports "a Fleiss’ kappa value of 0.7934 for five-way inter-annotator" agreement (char 1567), measured "over the 1,936 claims shows a score of 0.7934 in Fleiss κ" (char 28442); it reports FEVER's and CHEF's scores as "and CHEF are 0.6841 and 0.74, measured from 4% and 3%" (char 10406); and the authors reviewed "another 700 claims (2.33% of the dataset) for being reviewed" (char 29246), finding "We discovered that 84.4% of the claims are" / "annotated correctly with correct evidence sentences." (chars 24549, 24667) (`S091`). The 0.6841 matches the FEVER paper's own "tors achieving 0.6841 in Fleiss κ" (char 1854 of the `S086` capture).
- **PEOPLEPROFILES' labels are model labels, checked against a small human pilot.** The pilot is "of 160 body claims obtained from 10 randomly" selected entities (char 24245); bulk annotation is "Using GPT-4o-mini with the best prompt" from that pilot (char 22373) (`S090`). How well those model labels agree with people is a model-quality figure; the paper reports it (char 20954) and this lane leaves it to the evaluation lane (kengaku, `PROVINCE.md`).
- **The measured failure rates.** "∼22% of claims in Wikipedia lead sections" (char 1526) "are unsupported by the article body; (2) ∼30%" (char 1576) "of claims in the article body are unsupported" (char 1669) "by their (public) sources" (char 1723); the introduction says "ported by scrapable sources" (char 6302), and the study is limited to public sources because it is infeasible to reach those "to paywalled and print sources for all articles." (char 23238) (`S090`).

#### Inference

- **The support relation these sources define is evidential strength under supposition.** Both definitions condition on `supposing the contents of S` and ask whether that would give `greater reason to believe that c is true, all else equal` (`S090`); a passage ranked by its similarity to a claim measures a different relation from the one these sources name. In the lane's four-observation frame (`corpus/knowledge/claim-support-states-r24.md`) this is observation 3, and the lane's span check covers it at byte granularity only.
- **Absence of evidence is a recorded label measured against a fixed pool.** FEVER's evidence field carries `a [Annotation ID, Evidence ID, null, null] tuple if the label is NOT ENOUGH INFO` (`S085`), and CFEVER gives that label when, in its fact database, no factual sentence `could support or refute a claim, the claim was labeled as` (`S091`) — so a lane record that maps this outcome to `unresolved` should name the pool its search covered, as the FEVER pool is named by the lane's `corpus/knowledge/evidence-pool-versioning-r39.md`.
- **An empty evidence set is a permitted result of the selection step.** PEOPLEPROFILES' first step reads `Select 0, 1, 2, or *at maximum* 3 sentence(s)` (`S090`), and FEVER requires the sentences only `For the first two classes, the annotators also recorded the sentence(s)` (`S085`): a claim record that carries a verdict with zero evidence sentences is the third label, distinct from a record whose evidence field was left empty.
- **The three-way label is one shape across English and Chinese Wikipedia, and PEOPLEPROFILES departs from it.** FEVER's field reads `Can be one of SUPPORTS|REFUTES|NOT ENOUGH INFO` (`S085`) and CFEVER's abstract reads `Each claim in CFEVER is labeled as “Supports”,` (`S091`), while PEOPLEPROFILES grades support on `a scale from -1 to 1:` (`S090`) — a lane verdict vocabulary of three states can adopt the FEVER and CFEVER shape as is, and a scalar grade is a separate design.
- **A support verdict is as reliable as the labels behind it, and these figures bound it.** CFEVER's human agreement is `a Fleiss’ kappa value of 0.7934 for five-way inter-annotator` agreement and its authors' review reports `We discovered that 84.4% of the claims are` correctly annotated (`S091`). A single reader's support verdict in a lane record carries an error rate of this order that the record itself does not measure.
- **The two rates warn about the input, at the scope the paper measured.** The abstract's first rate, `∼22% of claims in Wikipedia lead sections`, is a hop inside one page with no citation in it; its second, `are unsupported by the article body; (2) ∼30%` continuing `of claims in the article body are unsupported` `by their (public) sources`, is the citation hop itself, measured on model labels (`S090`). A check that a cited document opens, or that a quoted span occurs in it, covers access and span identity; the second rate is the share of cited claims whose citation opens and still misses the support relation.
- **A figure carried across one citation hop matched its source here.** CFEVER restates FEVER's agreement as `and CHEF are 0.6841 and 0.74, measured from 4% and 3%` (`S091`), and the lane's FEVER paper capture carries the same 0.6841 (`S086`, Observation above) — the lane's one-hop chain rule applied to a figure, with the hop checked.

#### Unknown

- **Where the disagreement sits is not read.** Neither CFEVER's kappa nor PEOPLEPROFILES' alpha is broken down by label in the passages read, so whether the third label is the least reliable one is open; CFEVER's 84.4% does not separate label errors from evidence errors in the sentence read.
